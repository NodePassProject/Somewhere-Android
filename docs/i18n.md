# Internationalisation

The app follows the device language. Three locales ship:

| Locale | Resource directory | Base |
|---|---|---|
| English | `values/` | — |
| Simplified Chinese | `values-b+zh+Hans/` | — |
| Traditional Chinese | `values-b+zh+Hant/` | Taiwan usage |

## Match by script, not by region

Use the BCP 47 qualifier (`b+zh+Hans`), not the legacy region form
(`values-zh-rCN`). Region matching only covers the regions it names: `zh-rCN`
serves mainland China and leaves a Simplified-reading user in Singapore or
Malaysia on English. Script matching covers everyone who reads that script,
wherever they are. Available since API 21, so it is free at `minSdk` 26.

**Traditional Chinese uses Taiwan vocabulary.** `zh-Hant` is not Simplified with
converted characters — the words differ too: 軟體/軟件, 網路/網絡, 連線/連接. Taiwan
usage is the base because it is the larger reading population; a separate
`values-b+zh+Hant+HK` can be added later if Hong Kong usage justifies it. Never
generate one from the other by character conversion: it produces text that is
technically legible and obviously machine-made.

## Per-app language

Android 13 (API 33) lets a user set a language for one app in system settings.
Below that, the same thing is available through AndroidX. Support it either way
with `AppCompatDelegate.setApplicationLocales()` plus a `locales_config.xml`
listing the three locales — the backport handles pre-33 and the platform picks it
up on 33+.

Worth having because a person's device language and their preferred reading
language are not always the same, and this is exactly the kind of app someone
runs on a phone set to a language they did not choose.

## Machine identifiers stay English

**This is the rule that matters most here.** Translate the sentence, never the
identifier.

**Never translated** — these are values, not prose:

- The seven `SetupResult` names: `READY`, `INVALID_REQUEST`, `METADATA_CONFLICT`,
  `PAIR_TIMEOUT`, `FLOW_LIMIT`, `DIAL_FAILED`, `SESSION_REPLACED`,
  `INTERNAL_ERROR`
- Configuration parameters: `up`, `down`, `mux`, `alpn`, `sni`, `pin`, `rate`,
  `etar`, and the `nowhere://` scheme
- Carrier and transport names: `TLS/TCP`, `QUIC`, `MUX`
- Flow ids, session ids, hex fingerprints, timestamps

**Always translated** — the sentence that explains the identifier:

> `FLOW_LIMIT`
> This Portal is at its flow limit. Nothing is wrong with your setup — it is busy.

> `FLOW_LIMIT`
> 这个 Portal 的连接数已满。你的配置没有问题 — 它只是忙。

The reason is searchability, not laziness. A user reporting a problem should be
able to paste `DIAL_FAILED` and have it match the specification, the source, and
someone else's log from a different locale. Translating it to 拨号失败 breaks
every one of those links, and the person is no better off — the sentence beneath
it already told them what happened in their own language.

## Writing strings

- **No sentence assembly.** Never build a message by concatenating fragments;
  word order differs between languages. One `<string>` per complete sentence,
  with positional arguments: `<string name="quota_counted">%1$s of %2$s counted</string>`.
- **Use plurals**, even where English happens not to need them:
  `<plurals name="active_flows">`. Chinese has one form and English two; a
  hardcoded `"$n flows"` is wrong in both eventually.
- **Byte sizes, rates and durations are formatted, not concatenated.** Use
  `android.text.format.Formatter` and `NumberFormat` so grouping separators and
  units follow the locale.
- **Keep the identifier out of the translatable string.** Put `DIAL_FAILED` in
  the layout beside the message, not inside it, so a translator cannot
  accidentally localise it.
- **Mark what must not be translated**: `<string name="alpn_default"
  translatable="false">now/1</string>`.

## Layout under translation

Chinese runs roughly 30–40% shorter than English, which sounds harmless and is
not: a layout tuned to Chinese leaves English clipping, and one tuned to English
leaves Chinese looking sparse. Two habits cover it:

- Nothing gets a fixed width sized to its current text. Rows are flex with `gap`;
  labels wrap rather than ellipsise where the sentence carries meaning.
- The longest strings in the app are the seven rejection explanations. If they fit
  in both English and Chinese without the card growing, everything else will.

Chinese also needs a face with the glyphs — the app's Latin faces do not have
them. Fall back to the system CJK face rather than bundling one; a bundled CJK
font adds several megabytes for something every Android device already has.

## Not doing yet

- **RTL.** None of the three shipping locales is right-to-left, so it is not
  implemented. Use `start`/`end` rather than `left`/`right` in layouts anyway —
  it costs nothing now and is the whole job later.
- **Locale-specific number formatting in logs.** Log lines stay
  machine-formatted: a timestamp in a log is a value to compare, not prose.
